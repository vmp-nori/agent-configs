# Complete coverage reference

Use this reference to identify and replace the patterns catalogued in the supplied *BANNED: The Definitive Guide*. Treat every entry as a revision flag, not an automatic error: the source itself says that a single, deliberate use can work. Escalate only when a pattern is generic, interchangeable, decorative, or accumulated.

The universal correction is: name what happens, ground it in a particular person's perception or behavior, and show the resulting consequence. Prefer specificity over brevity, clarity over rhythm, and precision over convention.

## Part 1: constructions

1. **Sequential action pairs:** `X, then Y` stages movement without its emotional transition. Show intention, hesitation, or consequence.
2. **Vague interiority placeholders:** `something` shifts, tightens, flickers, or hardens in a body part. Name the feeling, thought, sensation, or observable response.
3. **Anthropomorphized silence:** silence, air, or atmosphere stretches, sits, hangs, presses, or grows. Show who waits, breaks, avoids, or pays a cost.
4. **Negation formula:** `not X, but Y`. Commit to the state directly.
5. **Triple-beat lists:** three emphatic nouns or fragments. Integrate the material or keep only the item that builds tension.
6. **Staccato verb fragments:** `[Verb]. [Verb]. [Verb].` Vary syntax and show causality.
7. **Conjunction starts:** habitual `And` or `But` sentence starts. Reserve them for earned separation; do not use them to manufacture cadence.
8. **Standalone Because fragments:** `Because [reason].` Integrate the reason or dramatize it.
9. **Hedged reactions:** a laugh, smile, sigh, or nod that `isn't quite` itself. Describe what the gesture actually does.
10. **Echo-line poetics:** adjacent mirrored lines that merely rephrase each other. Add information, escalation, complication, or contradiction.
11. **Mood-prop negation:** a character performs a meaningless action (`a drink he did not want`). Show motive and revelation, or cut it.
12. **Meta-narrative intrusion:** references to scenes, beats, real moments, or what is about to happen. Return to point of view and physical action.
13. **Atmospheric front-loading:** weather, skyline, architecture, or camera-pan set dressing before character presence. Start with action, decision, or perception.
14. **Gravitational metaphors:** attraction as pull, draw, orbit, gravity, or magnetism. Make attraction a choice with a motive.
15. **Suspension phrases:** words, questions, accusations, or silences that hang in the air. Show what follows.
16. **Impact similes:** shock as a blow, punch, slap, freight train, or knocked-out breath. Show the exact bodily and mental response.
17. **Ripple/stillness similes:** stone-in-water, ripples, shattered stillness, or cracked silence. Describe impact directly.
18. **Precision/control cluster:** surgical, practiced, economical, calculated, calibrated, methodical, clinical, or exacting action. Show the concrete movement and decision.
19. **Quality/texture defaults:** velvet, silk, steel, iron, granite, marble, or `silk over steel` for temperament. Find a real sensation or a particular texture.
20. **Blank desire statements:** want, need, ache, crave, or unbearable wanting without an object and motive. Specify what is wanted, why, and what it reveals.
21. **Articulation by proxy:** a look that says everything, no need for words, spoke volumes, said it all. Show the actual exchange and subtext.
22. **Hollow restraint:** held it together, swallowed it down, bottled it up, locked it away. State what is contained, how restraint works, and what failure would cost.
23. **Aestheticized damage:** drinking, sleeplessness, cigarettes, coffee, or self-destruction used as dark decoration. Dramatize its actual cost.
24. **Faux-intellectual aphorism:** portable, quotable profundity. Keep only a thought this person would genuinely have at this moment.
25. **Misapplied epic tone:** broke forever, changed everything, world-held-its-breath scale for a minor event. Match intensity to the event's real proportion.
26. **Elegant variation:** rotate demographic, role, age, or appearance descriptors to avoid names. Use names unless a descriptor establishes point of view, emotional distance, or power; `the man who used to be her husband` can do work, while rotating `the tall man` does not.
27. **False range construction:** `from X to Y` whose endpoints share no meaningful scale. Cut it unless a real continuum exists.
28. **Superficial analysis as narration:** trailing participles such as highlighting, underscoring, reflecting, emphasizing, showcasing, or symbolizing. Let the action carry the meaning.
29. **Negative parallelism:** `not only ... but`, `it's not about X, it's about Y`, `didn't just`, or `wasn't just`. State the point directly unless the second clause materially advances it.
30. **Simile-as-adverb:** `with the [noun] of someone [verb]ing`. Describe the actual person's behavior or state instead of inventing a comparison person.
31. **Trailing participle construction:** repeated main-clause-plus-`-ing` endings or trailing add-ons. One can work; two in a paragraph are noticeable; three consecutive instances create a generated-sounding rhythm. Reorder, split, or cut.

Supplementary quick-scan flags: `finally` as a transition, generic stillness endings, repeated domestic busywork, and repeated `, [verb]ing` or `, her [noun] [verb]ing` rhythms.

## Part 2: words and phrase clusters

Apply these clusters as search terms and density checks. Do not replace a term with a synonym that preserves the same underlying shortcut.

1. **Physical tells:** reflexive jaw, throat, breath, eyes, hands, spine, shoulders, heart, and chest reactions: tightening jaws; swallowed breaths; darkened eyes; clenched hands; stiffened posture; racing hearts. Replace with character-specific coping, thought, behavior, and consequence.
2. **Vague interiority:** `something` shifts, breaks, cracks, loosens, or changes; unnameable feelings; the weight of an emotion; waves that crash; emotions that coil; feelings in bones; active silence, air, or room. Name the exact inner or outer event.
3. **Default emotion descriptors:** raw, visceral, primal, bone-deep, soul-deep, marrow-deep, paper-thin, razor-thin, gossamer-thin, threadbare, frayed, or stretched to breaking. Name the actual emotion and pressure.
4. **Transition/beat placeholders:** long moments, beats, suspended moments, pauses, `finally`, and adjective-labelled silence or speech. Show what happens during the interval.
5. **Dialogue tags/modifiers:** adverbs after said or asked; voices that drop, tighten, go flat, break, harden, or soften; labels such as deceptively soft, falsely casual, or too casual. Let words and action carry delivery.
6. **Gaze/look descriptors:** assessing, appraising, cataloguing, tracking, watchful, guarded, shuttered, unreadable, inscrutable, too knowing, or seeing too much. Specify what the character notices, withholds, or does.
7. **Precision/control cluster:** surgical precision, practiced ease or efficiency, economical movement, calculated, calibrated, methodical, clinical, exacting, meticulous. Show the particular action.
8. **Competence shorthand:** effortless, seamless, fluid, graceful economy, no wasted movement, efficient brutality, quiet competence, easy confidence, contained power, coiled energy, deceptive stillness. Dramatize capability through choices and results.
9. **Quality/texture defaults:** velvet, silk, steel, iron, granite, marble, and related material shorthand. Describe sensation or a distinct material experience.
10. **Temperature metaphors:** cold voice/gaze/tone, frozen, ice in veins, blood ran cold, warmth spreading, pooled heat, fire up the spine, molten feeling. Use specific sensation, emotion, or behavior.
11. **Impact/force phrases:** physical blow, gut punch, freight train, slap, knocked-out air. Show the direct reaction.
12. **Ripple/stillness phrases:** stone in water, ripples, shattered stillness, cracked silence. Give a unique anchor or describe the effect directly.
13. **Breaking/cracking metaphors:** cracked open, split open, broke loose, shattered fragile things, fractures, fault lines, cracks in foundations. Show the particular change.
14. **Anchor/tether/grounding metaphors:** anchored, tethered, grounded, moored, centered, rooted, held in place, kept sane/present. Show stability as a concrete chosen connection or action.
15. **Edge/precipice metaphors:** on the edge, precipice, one word from falling, barely holding on, hanging by a thread. State the actual pressure and failure point.
16. **Sound/silence phrases:** words falling into silence, quiet swallowing speech, deafening or roaring silence, quiet pressing in, things hanging in air. Show the actors' response.
17. **Time/moment phrases:** time stretched, moments crystallized, suspended amber, frozen place, world narrowed, everything fell away, world held breath. Render concrete consciousness and events.
18. **Permission/allowance constructions:** allowed, let, gave permission, permitted a luxury, finally let go. Show what restraint was and what changes when it drops.
19. **Realization/understanding phrases:** clicked into place, pieces slotted, understanding dawned, clarity crashed, truth settled, crystallized. Dramatize the reasoning and consequence.
20. **Danger/threat descriptors:** lethal grace, predatory stillness, coiled to strike, dangerous edge, contained violence, quiet menace, soft threat, promise of violence. Show conduct, motive, capability, or threat.
21. **Space/presence phrases:** commanded, filled, dominated, or sucked air from a room; demanded attention; charismatic gravity; magnetic and impossible to ignore. Show how others' behavior changes.
22. **Intimacy/connection phrases:** space between, closed distance, breached defenses, slipped past guard, under skin, burrowed, took root, settled into cracks. Show specific relational choice and interaction.
23. **Possession/claiming phrases:** staked a claim, marked, branded, written into skin, carved into bones. State the connection and why it matters.
24. **Emotional-state shorthand:** tamped down, locked down, held in check, barely leashed, white-knuckled control, iron grip, ruthlessly suppressed, shoved down, compartmentalized. Show the mechanism, leak, and cost.
25. **Transition verbs:** shifted, settled, flickered, softened, hardened, sharpened, gentled, eased, tightened, loosened. State exactly what changed.
26. **Faux-edgy banter:** generic `you're impossible`, `you're trouble`, `you're the worst`, `you're ridiculous`, `you love it`, and similar stock sparring. Root dialogue in shared history and distinct voices.
27. **Cinematic wallpaper:** spilling or pooling light, playing shadows, skyline, floor-to-ceiling windows, sharp relief, neon glow, ozone, humming city. Retain setting only when a character's attention makes it matter.
28. **Description cliches:** ornamental eyes, porcelain or alabaster skin, mane-like hair, columns of neck, delicate wrists, sharp facial planes, jaw lines, broad shoulders, lean muscle. Choose individuating details.
29. **Domestic/clothing filler:** generic clothes and neutral chores, drinks, reading, tidying, or cleaning that do not create friction, decision, risk, or function. Cut or make them do work.
30. **Religious expressions:** generic invocations used as intensity markers. Use a character-specific expression or remove it.
31. **Ending cliches:** `for now, that was enough`, `it was a start`, `they would figure it out`, `nothing would be the same`, `everything had changed`. End on action or consequence.
32. **Miscellaneous high-frequency abstractions:** cut, slice, or pierce through; worm, claw, or fight a way to the surface; barely contained; held at bay; architecture, geometry, calculus, mathematics, or grammar of an emotion/relationship. Replace pseudo-intellectual framing with experience.
33. **AI vocabulary cluster:** abstract nouns such as tapestry, landscape, interplay, intricacies, nuance, dynamics, framework, paradigm; verbs such as delve, foster, garner, underscore, showcase, highlight, emphasize, align, enhance, navigate, unpack; inflated adjectives and adverbs such as pivotal, crucial, vibrant, multifaceted, profound, compelling, poignant, evocative, notably, ultimately, fundamentally, inherently, undeniably. Prefer plain, scene-specific words.
34. **Narrator-as-analyst phrases:** actions that highlight, underscore, reflect, emphasize, showcase, symbolize, illustrate, or demonstrate their own meaning. Remove the commentary; strengthen the action.
35. **Legacy/importance puffery:** testament, reminder, enduring legacy, lasting impact, indelible mark, profound heritage, vital role, paramount importance, cannot be overstated. Demonstrate consequence.
36. **Promotional/brochure language:** nestled, in the heart of, boasts, stunning, breathtaking, captivating, rich tapestry, vibrant culture, bustling, picturesque, idyllic. Replace promotional language with precise observation.
37. **Familiarity/routine cluster:** familiar, usual, rhythm, routine, ritual, comfortable routine, familiar pattern. Name the actual repeated behavior or cut it.
38. **Time-skip fillers:** passed in a blur, weeks flew by, time slipped away, before they knew it, lost track of time, days blended, time lost meaning, came and went. Show consequential elapsed time or cut directly.

## Part 3: operating framework

Use these checks across every category:

- **Accumulation test:** assess repetition over the recent pages and across characters. The source identifies density and interchangeability, not an isolated word, as the real problem.
- **Physical-tell test:** identify this character's stress outlet, speech change, posture, or displacement activity.
- **Interiority test:** identify what happens, what an observer sees, and what the character feels or thinks.
- **Metaphor test:** keep a comparison only if it clarifies a shared property rather than decorates.
- **Dialogue test:** remove the modifier; if tone disappears, revise the line or surrounding action.
- **Transition test:** show who acts or yields, what the wait costs, and what changes.
- **Opening/ending test:** start with character action or perception; end on action, decision, or consequence.
- **Final tests:** test every scene for specificity, interchangeability, consequence, decoration, and earnedness.

## Part 4: mature-fiction scene patterns and requirements

Apply this section only to adult fictional intimacy when the request is otherwise appropriate. Its craft rules do not override safety: require clear, affirmative, ongoing consent; never infer consent from silence, passive compliance, or positioning.

1. **Euphemism and romance fluff:** avoid sanitized anatomy, vague function language, and transcendent soul-union shorthand. Prefer direct but non-gratuitous language tied to action and perception.
2. **Religious framing:** avoid worship, sacredness, and generic invocations used as intensity decoration. Use character-specific expression instead.
3. **Decorative sweat:** avoid glistening, dewy, and generic perspiration shorthand. Describe observable heat, breath, strain, or touch only when it matters.
4. **Pornographic exaggeration:** avoid theatrical force, stock sounds, implausible bodily reactions, impossible fluid volume, and generic touch or depth claims. Keep anatomy and consequence credible.
5. **Generic vocal/aftermath tags:** avoid repeated animalistic sound labels and adjective-only intensity claims. Show condition, recovery, comfort, or distance through concrete behavior.
6. **Implausible choreography:** avoid unsupported positions, impossible leverage or grips, and instant physical accommodation. Account for angle, weight, fatigue, flexibility, support, and cleanup where relevant.
7. **Dark-romance substitutions for psychology:** do not use ownership, humiliation, punishment, training, or dominance aesthetics as a substitute for character, trust, and consent.
8. **Racial fetishization:** reject food-colour comparisons, racialized sexual stereotypes, animalizing language, and ethnicity-based stock metaphors.
9. **Abstract or therapeutic dialogue:** do not make characters lecture about their dynamic, power, or relationship theory during action. Keep dialogue short, immediate, character-specific, and connected to consent or the moment.

For every mature-fiction scene, establish a clear point of view, circumstance, narrative function, and visible change in trust, knowledge, relationship, or power. Keep voice distinct; make physical reality plausible; show practical aftermath where it matters; and end on a meaningful action, decision, interruption, touch, rupture, or consequence rather than a fade-out, theme statement, or static summary.

Before drafting, identify what will change, where bodies and objects are, what each person wants, how consent is communicated, and what ending action will close the scene. After drafting, scan for the listed patterns; verify consent, realism, consequence, distinct voices, and a non-cliche ending.
