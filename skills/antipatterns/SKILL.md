# Antipatterns

Write prose that earns its effects through specificity, character perception, action, and consequence. Treat this as a craft guide, not a detector-evasion technique: do not claim that any edit will make text undetectable as AI-written.

## Draft or revise

1. Identify the piece's speaker, audience, purpose, point of view, and desired level of formality. Preserve an intentional existing voice unless the user asks to change it.
2. State or infer the concrete situation: who is present, what they want, what obstructs them, and what changes.
3. Draft direct, scene-specific language. Prefer a precise observation, thought, choice, or consequence over a familiar flourish.
4. Review for repeated defaults and rewrite only the instances that are generic, interchangeable, decorative, or accumulated. Do not mechanically ban a construction merely because it appears once.
5. Make every revision add information, sharpen perception, advance action, or clarify consequence. Cut a line when it does none of these.

When revising a passage that prominently uses a flagged pattern, replace the underlying shortcut rather than swapping in a near-synonym. For example, do not turn a silence that "stretches" into a silence that "holds"; show the people waiting, avoiding, speaking, or acting. Do not swap one generic physical tell for another, such as replacing a tightened jaw with a jaw that "works." Do not retain a static ending if the task is to improve a stillness ending. Make an exception only when the user asks to preserve the original wording or the construction is demonstrably intentional and earns its place.

## Core rules

- Name the actual emotion, motive, sensation, or exchange. Do not hide it behind vague placeholders such as "something shifted" or "a look that said everything."
- Keep narration inside the scene or a character's perception. Do not add editorial explanations that announce significance.
- Give each character distinct stress responses, diction, and physical habits. Do not reuse the same jaw, breath, gaze, throat, or hand cues for everyone.
- Make figurative language clarify a particular experience. Replace dead metaphors and familiar emotional shorthand with direct, grounded description.
- Vary sentence rhythm for meaning, not decoration. Avoid repeated formulae, staged fragments, and parallel lines that only restate themselves.
- Open scenes with character presence, action, or a decision. Let setting arise through what matters to that character.
- End scenes on an action, decision, changed relationship, or visible consequence. Do not manufacture gravity through suspended silence or summary.
- Use names by default. Use a descriptor only when it establishes point of view, emotional distance, or a shift in power.

## Revision pass

Run the five tests below. Read [references/banned-patterns.md](references/banned-patterns.md) before a full revision or critique. It covers every named construction and lexical category in the supplied guide, plus its mature-fiction-specific categories, checks, and corrective actions.

1. **Specificity:** Could this line appear unchanged in another story or for another person? If so, make it particular or cut it.
2. **Interchangeability:** Could any character make this gesture or use this phrase? If so, ground it in this character's habits and stakes.
3. **Consequence:** Does the beat change an action, decision, relationship, or understanding? If not, develop it or remove it.
4. **Decoration:** Does the image clarify, or only sound literary? Keep only the clarifying image.
5. **Accumulation:** Has a familiar construction become a repeated default? Vary it only when the repetition is not deliberate or meaningful.

## Response format

- For a draft, return the requested prose without a craft lecture unless explanation is requested.
- For a revision, preserve the source's facts, voice, length, and structure unless the user authorizes broader changes.
- For a critique, quote or identify the pattern briefly, explain its concrete effect, and offer a specific alternative or question that helps the writer solve it.
- Apply the mature-fiction section only when the user asks for that kind of scene and the request is otherwise appropriate. Keep consent affirmative, ongoing, and clear; never treat silence, passive compliance, or positioning as evidence of consent.
